# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Nous Reader serves learners who need to understand an entire subject without losing the thread, especially people who benefit from an ADHD-friendly, step-by-step study flow. They may be working through technical, academic, or practical material on desktop or mobile and need explanations, sources, demonstrations, active pauses, and application work to remain part of one coherent lesson.

## Product Purpose

Nous Reader turns source material or researched topics into grounded, personalized courses. Success means that a learner can move through a whole subject with low navigation overhead, understand why each element is present, inspect the evidence behind it, and practice at the moment when the lesson has prepared them to do so.

## Brand Personality

Calm, exact, encouraging. The product should feel like a thoughtful tutor that organizes complexity without sounding simplistic, verbose, or performative.

## Anti-references

- A generic chat application where learning is reduced to an endless conversation.
- A file drive or content-creation suite that makes the learner manage artifacts instead of studying.
- Dashboard-like reading pages made from nested cards, detached widgets, and competing controls.
- Ambiguous custom affordances that require explanation before they can be used.
- Product copy that describes implementation details or advertises how the interface was generated.

## Design Principles

- Keep the learning flow continuous: explanations, media, sources, and active pauses belong inline at the point where they become useful.
- Make every control answer an immediate learner question; remove controls or decoration whose meaning is not self-evident.
- Preserve source fidelity: displayed clips, timestamps, captions, and citations must describe exactly what the learner can open or watch.
- Organize complexity progressively, without hiding precision or forcing the learner to reconstruct relationships across separate panels.
- Treat mobile usability, stable interaction patterns, and reading focus as core product behavior rather than later polish.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The interface should support keyboard navigation, visible focus, sufficient contrast, reduced-motion preferences, comfortable touch targets, and screen-reader semantics. Cognitive accessibility is central: labels should be concrete, state changes predictable, and dense material broken into meaningful steps without fragmenting the lesson.
