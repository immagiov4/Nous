# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Nous Reader is for learners who need to understand an entire subject without being overwhelmed by an undifferentiated mass of material. It is especially attentive to people with ADHD, learners studying from mobile devices, and anyone who benefits from a guided sequence, visible progress, and short, concrete next steps.

## Product Purpose

Nous Reader turns source material or a learning goal into a personalized, step-by-step course with lessons, quizzes, application exercises, contextual explanations, audio, and research-backed support. Success means the learner can resume reliably, always knows what to do next, and builds an accurate mental model of the whole subject rather than collecting disconnected answers.

## Brand Personality

Calm, rigorous, encouraging. The product should feel like a patient expert tutor: intellectually serious without being intimidating, supportive without becoming childish, and focused without becoming sterile.

## Anti-references

- A generic chat application where the learning path disappears into message history.
- A file drive or document manager that leaves the learner alone with uploaded material.
- A content-creation suite optimized for producing assets rather than understanding a subject.
- A dense enterprise dashboard with competing metrics, panels, and controls.
- A gamified learning product that uses noise, urgency, or decorative rewards to capture attention.

## Design Principles

- Make the next meaningful learning action obvious while keeping the wider path available.
- Preserve continuity: saved work, progress, and session state must feel dependable.
- Reveal complexity progressively instead of presenting the whole system at once.
- Prefer concrete explanations, examples, and practice over generic encouragement.
- Keep multimodal tools subordinate to learning: use text, visuals, audio, and research only when they improve understanding.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA for contrast, focus, keyboard operation, and semantic controls. Treat cognitive accessibility as a core requirement: stable layouts, plain language, restrained motion, low visual noise, clear error recovery, and no reliance on color alone. Respect reduced-motion preferences and preserve full usability on small mobile screens.
