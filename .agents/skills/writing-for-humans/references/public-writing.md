# Public and persuasive writing

Establish the intended audience and what the text should help them understand
or choose, using the supplied brand voice, product facts and examples. An
educational post should explain its subject; a promotional page may persuade
through a supported benefit and a clear action. Let that purpose determine the
form instead of assigning every piece a conversion funnel.

For compact cards, choose one essential mechanism or observation and a question
when the format calls for reflection. Use the accompanying description for the
evidence, example and conditions that the card cannot hold. Each layer should
add information, with the title naming or opening the subject and the body
developing it. Preserve requested word limits and languages separately for
public copy and internal working notes.

For product copy, connect the feature to a concrete outcome supported by the
available evidence, making the action, price and commitment understandable.
Read supplied product-marketing context before asking for facts. Preserve
necessary conditions on availability, price and performance; use verified
statistics and authentic testimonials with their attribution. Match the call
to action to what the user will actually receive or do.

Compare against authentic house examples for rhythm, structure and density.
Questions, humor, highlights and alternative headlines earn their place when
they serve that format; an organization can speak plainly without invented
excitement or claims of perfection. Offer variants when requested or when a
real editorial choice remains, keeping explanations proportionate to the task.
