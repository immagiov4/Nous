# Analytical and project reports

Use this reference for analytical, academic, and project reports.

## Author, reader, and required structure

Choose who speaks before drafting. Use the analyst's position in an analytical
report and the requester's authorial voice when drafting for them. Refer to "the
student" or "the author" only when that person is actually the subject of analysis.

Preserve the assignment's sections, template fields, citation style, and required
disclosures. Develop the argument through evidence, interpretation, and
consequences, keeping methods where they explain a result or limitation.

Decide voice separately for each deliverable. A public page may speak as an
organization while its accompanying analysis speaks as an analyst. Place
methodological detail in the analysis when the page's reader needs activities,
results, or documents.

## Evidence and interpretation

Lead an analytical paragraph with the supported result and its meaning, then
develop the evidence and material conditions that limit the interpretation.
State each condition where it belongs, preserving distinct limits on scope,
causality, comparison, uncertainty, and verification.

Dates belong where they establish the period, evidence, or sequence. Replace
vague timing with a relevant date or remove it when timing contributes nothing.
Include future plans when they are authored or requested, identifying their status.

A claimed application of a method or theory needs an identifiable concept, the
choice it informed, and the resulting analysis or artifact. Read the relevant
material before claiming that connection, citing a section or page when useful.

Verify comparisons with actual examples. Identify each source artifact's author,
audience, and role before borrowing its presentation. Establish requirements
from the assignment or governing instructions, using examples as evidence of
writing choices.

Support personal experiences, difficulties, durations, and sequences with
confirmation or records. Retain required attribution and provenance, and name
tools when the discussion examines them or citation is required.

## Relevance and presentation

Keep detail that changes understanding, a decision, reproducibility, or
evaluation. Describe methods that explain a result or limitation and give
source credit in its appropriate location. Use captions to explain what a
figure contributes.

Preserve the author's position, uncertainty, and necessary qualifications.
Keep closely related reasoning together so paragraphs build an argument.
